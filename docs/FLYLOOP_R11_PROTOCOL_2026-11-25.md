# FlyLoop R11 — Preregistered real recovery-episode slow-to-fast teaching
**2026-11-25. Committed BEFORE R11 implementation, any R11 simulation, or outcomes.** DRAFT stacked on R10 PR #100. Original main, M0–M4, sealed M5.6, R1–R10 unchanged.

## Narrow test and epistemic caveats
R9 proved a genuinely fast 0.05-s neural motor bypass, but bodily direct reward worsened contacts. R10 showed a SLOW critic can teach a four-neuron FAST network exclusively through its past actions, but 963 successful general-movement lessons included only 35 obstacle-salient episodes, and lesson mismatch control beat matched training. R11 addresses the previously stated hypothesis: **the slow circuit must first experience and recognize a whole collision→movement→real release from contact recovery sequence, then teach the fast circuit an analog motor action associated with this episode at an actual post-contact sensory state.**

It is NOT a demonstration of independently invented fear, biological reflex, escape reflex, fight, survival drive, intelligence or evolution. The time window, trigger, exploration distribution, recovery criterion, slow critic score and supervised rule are all RESEARCHER-SPECIFIED. Temporal association of a recovery motor with the earlier contact state does NOT prove the credited motor caused the recovery. No imaginary motor physics, no world oracle, path map, food, threats or scripted turn away from walls. **No stopping or reversing added to motor plant**: physical incapacity to stop/reverse is a separate subsequent experiment.

### Slow learner + researcher-enabled contact exploration
- SLOW critic attached to the original 16 recurrent states and actual three local rays has **3 independent 28D LMS heads (84 trainable coefficients)**, initialized ZERO, outputs clipped [0,1], offsets [.50,.25,.00] estimating (i) next physically realized 4-microstep normalized progress, (ii) any real macro contact, (iii) eventually verified subsequent recovery after a real contact. Model scores only real CURRENT local-sensor and original slow-neuron features plus ONE of five R7 local analog motor candidates (base; ±.35 steering; ±.20 throttle) at macro boundaries. Locked researcher-given score **progress −1.2×contact +0.7×recovery**; no future physics of alternative commands.
- Every macro the SLOW circuit first emits original two continuous motors. During TRAIN, before t=20 it always executes base. From t=20, every fourth macro (t divisible by 4) slow exploration draws uniformly from the five LOCAL motor candidates using independent seeded RNG. During any macro following an actual physical contact, a **12-macro contact-focused exploration window** begins or refreshes at REAL contact onset and the SLOW circuit instead draws BOTH motors uniformly in [-1,+1] from another seeded independent RNG, until 12 original macros elapse; draws are never guided left/right or away from wall. Contact-focused exploration enables the slow learner to physically TRY diverse actual actions, not to know their effect in advance. Explicitly label it a researcher-specified exploration trigger, NOT a learned reflex.
- Two critic heads (progress and contact) train online with LMS rate **.014**, clip [-2,2], only after the FULL actually executed macro. The third delayed "recovery" head trains at the END of a macro only once a real contact-recovery window completes, using the PRE-action 28D slow state+actual motor features of the macro at collision onset and the eventual truly observed 0/1 resolution. No rewinding slow neural state, no future label at choice time. All critic weights FREEZE before macro160 and remain fixed during evaluation.
- **Recovery detection of physical experience**: when a REAL physical microcontact occurs and no open window exists, start a 32-actual-microstep (1.60 s) episode; store first contact's source macro and the local 4-unit fast recurrent hidden state at the **FIRST physical microstep immediately AFTER actual impact** (not an anticipated contact). A recovery is VERIFIED if, within the same 32-microstep interval, the body completes **four consecutive actual microsteps with zero contact AND physically moves at least 0.035 world units** across those four microsteps. The taught target is the **SLOW-issued analog motor at the FIRST of these four verified contact-free microsteps**, which was actually physically executed. Recovery episode count is one per opened contact window (no nested windows). A window expires as FAILURE after 32 microsteps without verified recovery. If the body remains in contact, another observed physical contact can initiate a fresh window; do not count repeated microcontacts within one window as separate successes. At actual end of life/training, unresolved windows contribute no success and are separately disclosed.
- Slow 3rd recovery head learns BOTH successes and 32-step failures using the original collision-onset slow preaction features and the real resolution target. Valid successful teaching examples are stored only after the whole physical event resolved; snapshots/labels remain tagged to exact same real living body and source microtick. At any time before recovery the action isn't known and cannot be used to guide a fast student.
- The 4-fast-neuron student has 7 actual R9 LOCAL inputs, R9 four recurrent states and the 10 initially seeded analog motor readout coefficients. **There is no direct fast damage/reward learning in ANY arm.** At macro160, AFTER every actual TRAIN episode is completed (and all retrospective recovery windows closed or marked unresolved), students consolidate the teaching dataset by sequential deterministic supervised two-motor tanh-delta LMS **rate .025**, coefficient bounds [-2,2], with FAST hidden state of that actual first postcontact microstep. Nothing is learned during postchange evaluation.
- NO fake or replayed physical trajectories: consolidation is retrospective supervised replay of ACTUAL buffered local fast states and REAL actually physically issued slow motors from that SAME life. It changes only student readout coefficients; fast recurrent/input/hidden parameters stay frozen. The slow critic's recovery predictions remain separate from the fast network and are not provided to it during action.

### Six strict controls (ALL experience identical 160-macro training world)
1. **slow_only:** trained slow critic moves the body; never allows fast actuation; fast readout remains untrained.
2. **fast_fixed:** same initialized 4-neuron fast trigger and motors but no supervised training.
3. **fast_generic_matched:** SAME EXACT contact-state input snapshots and EXACT COUNT of supervised updates as R11 true; motor labels are real **previous safe no-contact slow motor commands** from this individual's training history (cycled deterministically if needed). This is R10-like generic teacher knowledge, matched for training count and relevance of snapshot. If there are no safe examples, skip all fast update controls for that life and record failure.
4. **fast_recovery_true:** genuine verified recovery slow-issued motor paired with its own real first-postcontact fast hidden state.
5. **fast_recovery_shuffled:** exactly SAME count/order/contact-state snapshots and SAME MULTISET of real verified recovery motor labels but a deterministic seed-specific **cyclic derangement** of lesson labels when N≥2; when N<2, correct and sham necessarily coincide and must be disclosed, NOT counted as evidence of a sham difference. No future physical labels (all from completed past training episodes).
6. **fast_recovery_random:** SAME real contact snapshots, SAME number of supervised updates, independent seeded uniform motor targets in [-1,1]. This is a synthetic RANDOM LABEL negative control, not physically experienced teacher instruction; always disclose.

After macro160 all fast arms use identical R9 local last-contact-or-proximity>.82 trigger, two shared analog actuators, .05-second micro motor physics and R9 seeded independent σ=.12 fast action noise. Motor priority fast only while triggered; otherwise slow critic action. No alternate policy motor lookahead. Same-arm static/removal full prechange action/position histories MUST match bytewise.
